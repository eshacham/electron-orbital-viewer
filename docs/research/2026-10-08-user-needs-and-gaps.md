# Electron Orbital Viewer: what researchers, teachers and students need

Prepared 2026-10-08 for the owner. The app was reviewed from `README.md` (What it shows, Across every mode, Computed molecules, Share and export, Limitations), the roadmap and plans in `docs/superpowers/`, the on-demand worker (`tools/jobs/worker.py`, `tools/jobs/pubchem.py`), and the live published library data (`https://d3rhfcclqjt4tf.cloudfront.net/molecules/v2/<id>/meta.json`, fetched for H2O, CH4, NH3, H2CO, benzene and C2H4).

**Two corrections to the brief, checked against the code:**
- Atom mode's many-electron solver is **LDA (Dirac–Slater exchange + VWN5 correlation)**, not Hartree–Fock. Ionisation energies are ΔSCF with spin-polarised LDA (README "Solving the atom", "Limitations / Atom mode").
- Phase 7 is **planned but not built**: lessons, compare view, Methods & validation page, CITATION.cff/DOI, embed mode, the accessibility pass and offline PWA. `src/lessons`, `src/compare` and `public/methods.html` do not exist, and `src/a11y/` holds only `contrast.ts`.

Abbreviations: KS = Kohn–Sham; ESP = electrostatic potential; MO = molecular orbital; TS = transition state; PES = potential energy surface (in the scans context) or photoelectron spectroscopy (in the atoms context). Which one is meant is always stated.

## 1. Needs by audience (ranked), with evidence and how existing tools serve them

### 1A. Researchers and scientists (academic and industrial: drug discovery, materials, catalysis)

| R9 | **Provenance and reproducibility**: exact inputs, versions, machine-readable outputs; citable data | MolSSI QCArchive and QCSchema exist to "document computational contexts and results" for FAIR data (Smith et al., *WIREs Comput. | R11 | **Reaction paths**: TS optimisation, IRC, energy profiles (catalysis, mechanism) | Undergraduate and graduate TS/IRC labs ([Q-Chem lab](https://www.q-chem.com/learn/lab1c/), [Kwan SN2 handout](https://ekwan.github.io/pdfs/computations/2%20-%20Transition%20State%20for%20an%20SN2%20Reaction.pdf)). | R12 | **Methods that meet current best practice** (dispersion-corrected functionals, adequate basis sets) | Bursch 2022: "B3LYP/6-31G* suffers from severe inherent errors, namely missing London dispersion", and the functional should "always include a dispersion correction (D3/D4, VV10)". | Rowan defaults to modern composite methods (r²SCAN-3c, ωB97X-3c). |

**What researchers will not need from this app:** protein visualisation (Mol*/NGL already do it very well), docking, or a replacement for Rowan's breadth. The realistic niche is **trustworthy, zero-install visualisation and sharing of electronic structure, with real provenance**, plus a small set of well-validated workflows (R2–R5) and a viewer for researchers' own files (R10).

### 1C. Product developers (by extension)

These are people building on the app or embedding it: course-platform builders, publishers, and ML/data teams.

1. **Stable embed and URL API, with a licence**: an iframe snippet, a no-chrome mode, documented hash keys, and MIT/CC-BY. Evidence: LibreTexts and H5P embedding (above); the 3Dmol.js BSD licence is cited as a reason for adoption ([3Dmol LE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9416521/)).
2. **Machine-readable, standard outputs**: QCSchema JSON, molden, cube, xyz/sdf, CSV ([QCArchive](https://wires.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/wcms.1491)).
3. **A programmatic job API** (Rowan's API use is growing with agentic integrations, [survey](https://rowansci.com/blog/what-we-learned-from-our-user-survey)). This needs public on-demand compute first.
4. **Citable, versioned data with DOIs**: already planned (Zenodo, roadmap decisions 4 and 5).

## 4. Things these users would find misleading, insufficient or wrong

Ordered by how quickly an expert would notice.

1. **Orbital symmetry labels are in a subgroup, not the molecule's point group.** In the live data, CH₄ (Td) is labelled in D2, so its triply degenerate HOMO reads "1b1, 1b2, 1b3" instead of 1t₂. NH₃ (C3v) is labelled in Cs ("4a′" for the 3a₁ lone pair; "3a′/1a″" for the 1e pair). Benzene (D6h) is labelled in D2h ("1b2g/1b3g" for the 1e₁g HOMO; "2b1u/1au" for the 1e₂u LUMO). The app does disclose this ("Labels: irreducible representations of D2, the subgroup of Td the calculation uses", `MoleculeOrbitalList.tsx`). But every textbook and every PES-based teaching example (CH₄'s two PES bands, t₂ and a₁) uses the full-group labels, so a student cannot match them. The cause is a known PySCF limit ("supports ... D2h and its subgroups", [pyscf.org](https://pyscf.org/user/gto.html)). **Fix:** correlate the subgroup labels back to the full group by degeneracy and character projection (priority 1).

2. **The "LUMO" of several molecules is a basis-set artefact, not a σ\* orbital.** Live values at B3LYP/def2-TZVPD: H₂O "LUMO" 4a₁ at −0.28 eV; CH₄ "LUMO" 3a at **+0.27 eV**; NH₃ "LUMO" 5a′ at **+0.01 eV**; H₂CO 6a₁ at +0.15 eV. Positive-energy (and near-zero, diffuse-augmented) virtuals are discretised continuum or Rydberg-like states whose shape and energy depend on the basis set: "the ones above zero are spurious, since the positive orbital energies are artifacts of the basis" (van Meer, Gritsenko, Baerends, *JCTC* 2014, 10, 4432, [link](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/ct500727c)). See also "the LUMO is thus lost within a continuum" ([JPCA 2015, valence virtual orbitals](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jpca.5b06893)). A teacher showing "water's LUMO" will likely get a diffuse blob, not the textbook O–H σ\*. Adding diffuse functions (def2-TZVPD) fixed the dipoles but made this worse. **Fix:** flag positive and diffuse virtuals. Optionally compute virtuals for display in the non-augmented def2-TZVP basis, or show valence virtual orbitals (VVOs/IAO-projected), and say so.

3. **"HOMO–LUMO gap" is shown without saying it is neither the optical nor the fundamental gap.** KS gaps from B3LYP differ from both. The water HOMO is −8.8 eV against a measured first IE of about 12.6 eV, a typical B3LYP underestimate. Bredas, "Mind the gap!" (*Mater. Horiz.* 2014, 1, 17, [link](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2014/mh/c3mh00098b)) documents the confusion this causes in the literature. The panel does say "Kohn–Sham orbital energies ... Not ionisation energies", but the gap line itself has no caveat.

4. **"Validated" tier vs what was validated.** For molecules whose geometry is *taken* from CCCBDB experiment (water, for example), the reference rows compare bond length and angle against the same CCCBDB experiment. That is circular. Confirmed in `tools/molecules/build_library.py` (lines 212–214): the row's `app` value is measured from the shipped geometry and its `method` is `meta['geometrySource']`, i.e. "experiment (CCCBDB)". For those molecules the row is a transcription check, not a validation; it is a real check only for the seven B3LYP-optimised molecules. Only the **dipole** is an independent check, and orbitals and ESP are not validated at all; no experimental ESP exists. A researcher reading "Validated" on an orbital picture will over-trust it. **Fix:** name exactly what was validated next to the badge ("dipole within 10 % of experiment").

5. **On-demand ("Computed") results fall short of current best practice:**
   - Recipe A runs DFT on PubChem's 3D conformer, which PubChem itself says "is not at an energy minimum and may not represent the lowest energetic form" ([PubChem3D](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/release3d.html)). It is generated with MMFF94s *without Coulomb terms*, and only exists for ≤ 50 heavy atoms, ≤ 15 rotatable bonds and organic elements only.
   - Recipe B optimises at B3LYP/def2-SVP without dispersion and without a frequency check. The caveats say this honestly (`worker.py` CAVEATS), but Bursch et al. 2022 explicitly recommend always including D3/D4 and checking frequencies.
   - For flexible molecules (drug-like, the researchers' use case), a single unscreened conformer makes the ESP and dipole conformer-dependent. That is a scientific, not cosmetic, problem.
   - Acceptable while owner-only. Must be fixed before public use (priority 8).

6. **Atom mode's orbital energies vs what students compare them with.** The level diagram uses LDA eigenvalues. Students in AP/general chemistry compare against photoelectron spectra (AP Chem Topic 1.6), where LDA valence eigenvalues are 30–40 % too shallow (README: Ne 2p 37 % off). The app labels them correctly as "orbital energies", but offers no measured PES or per-subshell ΔSCF binding energies to look at instead. That invites a wrong comparison precisely in the course that uses it most (priority 4/9). The brief's own description of atom mode as "HF" shows how easily this gets misread: HF eigenvalues would be much closer via Koopmans; LDA's are not.

7. **Molecules mode's density surface is an enclosed fraction, while Bonds-mode density and the ESP surface are fixed-ρ.** The README documents this, but the conventional molecular "size" isovalue that researchers and textbooks use is ρ = 0.001–0.002 e/a₀³. Two different rules for "density" in adjacent modes will confuse comparisons. **Fix:** offer 0.002 e/a₀³ as the default in Molecules too, or label the fraction prominently.

8. **Hybrids drawn for hydrogen.** sp/sp²/sp³ built from hydrogen's 2s/2p are captioned "a basis choice, not a state", which is good. Teachers in the Grushow debate ([J. Chem. Educ. 2011](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/ed100155c)) will still want the counter-picture: methane's canonical MOs (a₁ + t₂, two PES bands) beside its localised (sp³-like) orbitals. The app has the data but cannot show localised orbitals (priority 7).

9. **Bonds mode mixes methods per picture**: CCSD(T) curves with B3LYP orbitals and densities. This is disclosed and acceptable, but an MO diagram drawn at a geometry from a CCSD(T) curve can be misread as a CCSD(T) orbital diagram; keep the method on the diagram itself. Also, **no vibrational levels or D0** means the D_e shown is not what experiment measures. Disclosed, but students will compare it with D0 tables (priority 3).

10. **Insufficient for researchers, not wrong:** no frequencies, conformers, solvent, charges, file import or molden export; owner-only compute; no DOI or licence. A researcher cannot use or cite results today.

**What experts will find credible, and should be kept:** stating the method on every number; refusing unbound anions; T1-diagnostic curve cut-offs; documented known misses (ozone); a fixed ESP colour scale; ΔSCF not eigenvalues for IEs; full input-script provenance; watertight-only STL export. These match or exceed the practices recommended in the education literature (Hinze 2013 on ESP scales; Autschbach 2012 on orbital caveats) and in QCArchive-style provenance.
