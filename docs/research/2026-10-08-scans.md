## 1. What scientists actually run

### 1.1 Rigid and relaxed scans

- **A rigid (unrelaxed) scan** moves one internal coordinate and freezes all
  the others, so each point is a single point. It is cheap and embarrassingly
  parallel. It gives an **upper bound**: torsional barriers come out too high,
  because nothing relaxes away from the clash. Jaguar's manual says rigid
  scans "are faster but do not give as good a picture of the minimum energy
  surface". Q-Chem offers it as `FROZEN_SCAN` with a Z-matrix input. Rigid
  scans are right where the frozen coordinates are the point, as in classic
  Walsh diagrams at fixed bond length, and trivially right for a diatomic,
  where rigid and relaxed coincide.
- **A relaxed scan** runs a constrained optimisation at each value: the
  scanned coordinate is fixed and everything else is minimised. Gaussian
  (`Opt=ModRedundant`, `D 1 2 3 4 S 36 10.0`), ORCA (`%geom Scan D 0 1 2 3 =
  180, 360, 19 end`), Q-Chem (`JOBTYPE = PES_SCAN`, `$scan tors …`), Psi4
  (a Python loop over `optking` `frozen_dihedral`), Jaguar ("Relaxed
  Coordinate Scan"), WebMO ("Coordinate Scan") and Rowan all default to it.
  It is what people mean by "a torsion scan".
- **Hysteresis.** A one-directional sequential relaxed scan inherits
  whatever the previous point's other coordinates were. A second torsion,
  a ring pucker or a hydrogen bond can stay "stuck" and then snap back, so
  the profile is discontinuous, and forward and backward sweeps disagree.
  TorsionDrive (Qiu et al., JCP 2020) fixes this with **wavefront
  propagation**: each grid point is re-optimised from its neighbours until
  no lower energy appears. OpenFF/QCArchive use it for all their training
  data, and Rowan enables it by default ("eliminating the bizarre memory
  effects that plague many torsional scans"). Rowan's own docs add that it
  "can improve a scan but does not guarantee the global minimum at every
  point".

### 1.2 Torsion scans for drugs and force fields

- **OpenFF standard:** 1-D dihedral grid, **24 points at 15°** over
  [−180, 180), TorsionDrive + geomeTRIC, **B3LYP-D3(BJ)/DZVP**. Behara et
  al. (JPCB 2024) benchmarked it against CCSD(T)/CBS: **RMSE 0.52
  kcal/mol, within 0.1 of the best functional tested**. OpenFF's target is
  0.5–1 kcal/mol, and they note def2-TZVP costs ~4× DZVP.
- **Schrödinger** (Jaguar relaxed scans feeding Force Field Builder for
  OPLS4) and **Rowan** (relaxed scans with wavefront propagation, bond,
  angle or dihedral, optional 2-D grid; "10 points in each dimension give
  100 grid points") present the same product: a 1-D profile or 2-D
  surface, plus a structure per point.
- **Genentech's 62-fragment torsion benchmark** (Sellers et al., JCIM
  2017; CCSD(T)/CBS reference) is the standard drug-fragment test.
  Aryl–amide and biaryl torsions are the hard cases for force fields, and
  the classic reason people run QM torsion scans at all.

### 1.3 Bond-dissociation curves

- **Closed-shell restricted methods fail on homolytic dissociation.** RHF
  and RKS dissociate H₂ to a 50:50 mix of H·H· and H⁺H⁻, far too high in
  energy. Past the **Coulson–Fischer point** the RHF solution becomes
  unstable, and an unrestricted solution with broken spin symmetry is lower
  and dissociates correctly, at the price of spin contamination
  (⟨S²⟩ → 1 for the H₂ singlet).
- **Practical DFT recipe:** at each point run UKS from a symmetry-broken
  guess (`init_guess_breaksym`) and check stability (`mf.stability()`).
  Keep the lower solution and report ⟨S²⟩. In PySCF 2.8 this is
  `dft.UKS(mol)` with `init_guess_breaksym=1`, plus `mf.stability()` →
  re-run from the unstable mode.
- **Single-reference CCSD(T) fails further out**, where the T1 diagnostic
  explodes. That is the rule Bonds mode already enforces: ship points only
  up to T1 > 0.02/0.03, non-convergence, or turnover. Beyond it you need
  multireference methods (CASSCF/NEVPT2/MRCI). PySCF has them, but they
  need an active space per molecule, so they cannot be automatic.
- The Morse fit (D_e, a, R_e → ω_e) on top of a dissociation curve is
  standard teaching material.

### 1.4 2-D scans, reaction paths, NEB and IRC

- **2-D scans cost points²:** 24 × 24 = 576 constrained optimisations.
  ORCA's `Simul_Scan` (all coordinates moved together) exists precisely to
  avoid that. geomeTRIC builds a **full Cartesian-product grid** from
  several `$scan` lines and walks it row-major, so each row jumps back to
  the start. That jump is a warm-start and hysteresis problem.
- **NEB, TS and IRC** are reaction-path tools. geomeTRIC 1.1 has NEB, TS
  optimisation and IRC. TS needs a finite-difference Hessian (6 × N_atoms
  gradients) or PySCF's analytic DFT Hessian. A relaxed bond scan is the
  poor man's reaction coordinate, and ORCA's manual recommends "the relaxed
  surface scan with a fast method and smaller basis set", then single
  points at a higher level. That is the two-tier pattern adopted here.

- an **energy profile** (CSV and plot), **relative to the minimum**, in
  **kcal/mol and kJ/mol**;
- **minima, maxima and barrier heights** read off it;
- **one structure per point** (multi-frame XYZ);
- an **animation** through the points;
- for force-field work, **the constraint value actually achieved**
  alongside the target.

## 3. Method choice

### 3.1 Plain B3LYP/def2-TZVPD is the wrong default for torsions

- **Without dispersion,** B3LYP's conformational energies are poor.
  GMTKN55 (Goerigk, Grimme et al. 2017) does not recommend plain B3LYP, and
  its D3(BJ) correction is standard practice for conformers. OpenFF's
  production level is B3LYP-**D3(BJ)**.
- **For relative torsional energies a DZ/SVP basis is adequate.** OpenFF's
  B3LYP-D3(BJ)/DZVP has 0.52 kcal/mol RMSE against CCSD(T)/CBS. def2-TZVPD's
  diffuse functions buy almost nothing for neutral torsion profiles and cost
  2–3× (caffeine: N 614 vs 494 for TZVP).
- **D3(BJ) in PySCF 2.8** is `mf.disp = 'd3bj'` (or `xc = 'b3lyp-d3bj'`),
  and needs the `pyscf-dispersion` package. Version 1.5.0 ships
  **linux x86-64 and linux aarch64 wheels, and no macOS wheel.** The worker
  image (Fargate ARM64 and OrbStack) is fine. The Mac `tools/molecules`
  venv would need it built from source, or must skip D3 in local tests.
- **Decision:**
  - Relaxed-scan geometries and the profile: **B3LYP-D3(BJ)/def2-SVP**
    (density fitting, def2-universal-jkfit).
  - Profile refinement: **B3LYP-D3(BJ)/def2-TZVP** single points (also
    density-fitted) on each optimised geometry, when the sizing rule fits
    them under the scan's ceiling. Otherwise the profile stays at SVP and
    says so.
  - This is the two-tier pattern ORCA's manual recommends. It also keeps
    the scan consistent with recipe B, which already optimises at SVP.
  - Recipe B should get D3(BJ) too, at the next sizing version. Its UI
    caveat already admits "no dispersion correction".
- **Density fitting** changes caffeine's B3LYP/def2-SVP **absolute** energy
  by 5×10⁻⁵ Eh (0.03 kcal/mol; measured here). Relative energies along a
  scan change far less. That is irrelevant at the 0.5 kcal/mol accuracy
  level, and the SCF ran **3.4× faster** (below).

### 3.2 When CCSD(T) is worth it

- **It is worth it** for benchmark-quality small-molecule curves (the
  bond-type catalogue, butane's barrier to ±0.1 kcal/mol, H₂/N₂/CO curves
  up to their T1 limit). Bonds mode already does exactly this offline.
- **It is not worth it** for drug fragments. CCSD(T)/cc-pVDZ on caffeine is
  ~35 min per point on L, and is not more accurate for torsions than
  B3LYP-D3(BJ)/SVP, because DZ CCSD(T) has large basis-set error.
  CCSD(T)/cc-pVTZ on caffeine is ~1 day per point. PySCF has no
  DLPNO-CCSD(T) to make it cheaper.
- **Measured here** (PySCF 2.8 RCCSD(T), frozen core, single thread on the
  Mac):

| System | N | o (active) | v | CCSD (10 it) | (T) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ethane cc-pVTZ | 144 | 7 | 135 | 21.0 s | 32.8 s |
| Propane cc-pVTZ | 202 | 10 | 189 | 83.3 s | 110.9 s |

- **The resulting rates:** (T) ≈ 1.15×10¹⁰ o³v⁴ per second per Mac core,
  and CCSD ≈ 1.5×10¹⁰ o²v⁴ per second per iteration.
- **The AWS L conversion is anchored on the owner's caffeine step:** the
  same caffeine B3LYP/def2-SVP SCF+gradient takes 140 s here on one thread
  (13 cycles, in-core) against 31 s (mean) to 39 s (slowest) per step in
  the owner's L job. That makes **L ≈ 4× this Mac's single thread**.
  Coupled cluster is BLAS-bound and probably scales better, so treat these
  as ±2×. A probe should confirm before any constants are fixed.

  "CCSD(T) ∼1500 MOs" on "a node of a cluster, or a few dozen cores". Its
  example (Table III) is H₅₀/cc-pVQZ (25 occupied, 1472 virtual):
  8355 s for one AO-driven CCSD iteration on 28 cores. Those are
  low-occupied hydrogen chains. Organic molecules hit the o³ wall far
  sooner, as the table shows.
- **Verdict on "optional CCSD(T)" in v1: no.**
  - It needs its own sizing law and probe.
  - It needs the T1/turnover validity machinery ported from
    `tools/molecules/generate.py`.
  - It is useful only up to ~4 heavy atoms, where the offline Bonds
    pipeline already produces it.
  - Ship it in v2 as "CCSD(T) single points on the scan's DFT geometries"
    (CCSD(T)//B3LYP-D3(BJ)/SVP). Gate it at **o³v⁴ ≤ 1×10¹³** (≤ ~6 min
    per point on L). Apply Bonds mode's T1 cutoff and caption verbatim.

## 4. PySCF 2.8 and geomeTRIC 1.1: mechanics and pitfalls

### 4.1 Mechanics

- **Constraints file** (geomeTRIC docs; parser read in
  `geometric/prepare.py`):
  - **Modes:** `$freeze` (hold the current value), `$set` (drive to a
    target and hold it), `$scan` (a list of targets).
  - **Lines:** `distance i j [Å]`, `angle i j k [deg]`,
    `dihedral i j k l [deg]`, `xyz i`, `rotation …`. **Atom indices are
    1-based.**
  - **Scan syntax:** `dihedral 1 2 3 4 0.0 180.0 13` gives **13 points
    including both ends** (15° spacing). `12` would give 16.36°.
- **From PySCF:** `pyscf.geomopt.geometric_solver.kernel(mf,
  constraints='file', maxsteps=…, callback=…)` returns `(converged, mol)`.
  `mf.Gradients().optimizer(solver='geomeTRIC').kernel({'constraints': …})`
  is the same thing. Useful extra options: `enforce` (exact constraint
  satisfaction once within 0.1 of the target; it helps slow convergence)
  and `conmethod=1` (better in repulsive regions).
- **Dihedrals are periodic internally** (`isPeriodic = True`, ±2π
  differences removed), so the scan crosses ±180° without wrapping
  trouble. A scan of −180 to 180 inclusive duplicates the same geometry at
  both ends, so use 24 points at 15° from −180 to 165, or treat the
  duplicate as a free hysteresis check.
- **`$set` drives the coordinate from wherever it is.** A large jump
  (e.g. 180° → 0° in one go) means many steps and possibly a different
  basin. Hence small increments and a warm start.

### 4.2 Pitfalls reproduced for this report

1. **`symmetry=True` silently defeats a dihedral scan.**
   - What happens: PySCF's geomeTRIC engine pins `mol.symmetry` to the
     starting `topgroup`, and **symmetrises every geometry** onto it.
   - Reproduced on trans-H₂O₂ (C2h) with `$set dihedral 3 1 2 4 120.0`:
     - `symmetry=False` gives **converged=True, H–O–O–H 120.0°**;
     - `symmetry=True` gives **converged=True, H–O–O–H 180.0°**. That is
       success reported for a constraint never reached.
   - The worker's `input_template.py` builds `mol` with `symmetry=True`.
   - **Scans must build the optimisation `mol` with `symmetry=False`**,
     then detect the point group per point (it changes along a scan:
     butane C2h → C2 → C2v) only for orbital labels.
   - **Always check the achieved constraint value** against the target
     (|Δ| < 0.1°, 0.001 Å), and fail that point if it misses.
2. **`maxsteps` counts gradient calls across the whole `$scan`, not per
   point**, and a miss aborts the entire scan.
   - Reproduced: a 7-point H₂O₂ `$scan` needs 38 gradient calls. With
     `maxsteps=12` it returns `converged=False` after 12 calls, partway
     through, with only one geometry back and no per-point results.
   - `$scan` through PySCF also writes `scan-final.xyz` **into the current
     directory**, and returns only the last `mol`.
   - **So the worker should not use `$scan`.** It loops itself, one `$set`
     optimisation per point, with a per-point `maxsteps` (50). It writes
     each point's energy, geometry, steps and achieved value to the
     checkpoint as soon as the point finishes.
3. **Density-fitted gradients crash with the worker's lock** (PySCF 2.8.0
   with NumPy 2.5.3).
   - Error: `pyscf.lib.einsum` → `ValueError: not enough values to unpack
     (expected 4, got 3)` in `df/grad/rhf.py`.
   - Cause: NumPy 2.4 changed `einsum_path`'s contraction tuples. PySCF
     master handles it ("numpy 2.4.0 changes the einsum_path APIs"); the
     latest release is 2.14.0.
   - Recipe B's non-DF gradients are unaffected, which is why the owner's
     caffeine optimisation ran.
   - **Fix before scans use DF:** pin `numpy<2.4` in `requirements.lock`
     (the minimal change), or upgrade PySCF (a provenance change). Add a
     DF-gradient smoke test.
4. **Linear angles.**
   - A constrained dihedral whose A–B–C or B–C–D angle becomes linear
     raises `LinearTorsionError`.
   - Angles ≥ 175° degrade convergence (geomeTRIC docs).
   - So relaxed angle scans stop at 175°, and **Walsh-type bends to 180°
     run rigid**.
   - Refuse dihedral scans whose central atoms include an sp centre.
5. **Warm start.**
   - Reuse one `mf.Gradients().as_scanner()` across points: the scanner
     keeps the last density matrix as the next guess. A fresh `make_dft()`
     per point (as recipe B does) cold-starts every SCF from minao.
   - The owner's caffeine probe shows what that costs: a cold first step
     took 14 cycles, warm steps 10–11.
6. **2-D `$scan` grids are row-major products, not serpentine.** Each new
   row starts from the far end of the previous row, a big geometry jump.
   This is one more reason to defer 2-D.
7. **Wrong basin, silently.** A point can relax into a different
   conformer of another torsion. A one-point overlap between chains, plus
   a forward/backward check on the first and last points, detects it.
   Flag points that disagree by > 0.5 kcal/mol. A full wavefront pass is
   v2.
8. **Dissociation.**
   - RKS at stretched geometries converges to the wrong (ionic-mixed)
     state without complaint.
   - Bond scans use UKS + `init_guess_breaksym` + `mf.stability()`, and
     report ⟨S²⟩.
   - Run them from R_e outward, warm-started, so the broken-symmetry
     solution is followed continuously.

### 4.3 Measured: a relaxed butane torsion scan

This run was done for this report. It is n-butane, B3LYP/def2-SVP (no D3,
because `pyscf-dispersion` has no macOS wheel), grid level 3, on the Mac with
one thread. It uses a `$set` loop with `symmetry=False`; each point starts
from the previous optimised geometry, with geomeTRIC's default convergence.

**Run 1: 30° spacing.**

| Target | Achieved | ΔE (kcal/mol) | Reference CCSD(T) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 180 (anti) | 180.0 | 0.000 | 0 |
| 150 | 150.0 | 1.646 | |
| 120 (eclipsed) | 120.0 | 3.463 | 3.30 (trans–gauche barrier) |
| 90 | 90.0 | 1.881 | |
| 60 (≈ gauche) | 60.0 | 0.845 | 0.59–0.63 (gauche) |
| 30 | 30.0 | 3.212 | |
| 0 (syn) | 0.01 | 5.506 | 4.89–5.46 (syn) |

- All 7 points took ~20.5 min in total on one Mac thread.
- The last point (30° → 0°) took **21 steps, 185 s (8.8 s per step)**.
- Without dispersion, plain B3LYP puts gauche ~0.25 kcal/mol too high.
  That is the known missing-dispersion error, and the reason the default
  is D3(BJ).
- A 30° grid also misses where the gauche minimum and the barriers
  actually are. Hence 15° as the default.

**Run 2: 15° spacing.**

| Target | Steps | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 180 | 5 | 48.7 s (the start geometry was already nearly optimal) |
| 165 | **24** | 221 s (0.464 kcal/mol) |
| 150 | **19** | 174 s (1.648 kcal/mol, the same as run 1's 150°: no hysteresis) |

**Conclusion: plan for ~20 steps per warm point** with geomeTRIC defaults,
not 5–10. Halving the increment barely reduces it.

- The cost is dominated by re-converging the soft methyl rotors to the
  default thresholds (gmax 4.5×10⁻⁴ Eh/bohr), starting from a fresh
  guess Hessian at every point.
- Two candidate levers, to measure in the v1 probe before relying on
  either:
  - looser convergence for scan points (geomeTRIC `convergence_set
    GAU_LOOSE`; energies converge much faster than geometries);
  - carrying the previous point's Hessian into the next optimisation.

| Owner's earlier direct L probe (cold first step) | | | 52.9 s |

- **L ≈ 4× this Mac's single thread** for a DFT step.
- A density-fitted DFT gradient is typically several times faster than a
  direct one, so the planning assumption is a **~3× faster step overall
  with DF**. It must be measured once pitfall 3 is fixed.

**Prices** (spec §7.5, per hour; S/M/L/XL):

| | S | M | L | XL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spot | $0.030 | $0.060 | $0.238 | $0.609 |
| On-demand | $0.093 | $0.187 | $0.746 | $1.905 |

The SCF speedup is `min(c,16)^0.867`: 1.82, 3.33 and 11.1. So per CPU-hour
**smaller sizes are ~25 % more cost-efficient**, and parallel children on M
are cheaper than one long L, provided the integrals still fit in core (the
in-core N limit is 260 on S, 320 on M, 465 on L, 655 on XL; sizing v4).

**Steps per point.**

- Measured here (§4.3): a warm-started relaxed butane point takes **19–24
  steps** at 15° or 30° spacing with geomeTRIC defaults. The first point
  from a reasonable geometry is a full optimisation (5 steps here;
  caffeine took 11).
- Plan: **20 steps per point**, with `min(10 + 2·atoms, 30)` for each
  chain's seed.
- Tighten it with the measured steps per chain (v5 already records cycles
  per step), and with the looser-convergence lever if the probe shows it
  is safe.

Sequential vs parallel:

- **Sequential with a warm start is both better and cheaper per point.**
  Fewer steps, fewer SCF cycles, and a continuous profile.
- **Fully parallel per point** (rigidly rotated starts) costs ~1.5–2× the
  steps, since each point starts further from its relaxed geometry. It
  invites wrong-basin points, and pays Fargate's ~1 min start-up and 60 s
  minimum billing 24 times. Fully parallel is only right for **rigid**
  scans.
- **Split into k ≤ 4 warm chains** when the sequential prediction
  × `TIME_HEADROOM` exceeds the per-child ceiling. Each chain is one Batch
  array child (`AWS_BATCH_JOB_ARRAY_INDEX` picks its arc). Its seed is the
  rigid rotation of the input to its first grid value. Each chain computes
  one extra point into its neighbour's arc, for the hysteresis check.
- Checkpointing per point makes **Spot safe** for each chain: a reclaim
  costs at most one point. So a checkpointed scan child should be allowed
  on Spot up to its 2 h ceiling. This is a deliberate exception to "Spot
  only ≤ 60 min", which exists because an uncheckpointed job loses
  everything on a reclaim.

### 5.2 C₆₀: the case for a smaller basis, not a longer ceiling

N for C₆₀ is 840 at def2-SVP, 1860 at def2-TZVP, 2220 at def2-TZVPD and
1800 at cc-pVTZ.

- **def2-TZVPD direct:** sizing v3/v4 says **18.0 h on XL**. The speedup
  saturates at 16 threads, so XL buys memory, not time.
- **PySCF's own benchmark, C₆₀/cc-pVTZ HF on 16 cores:**
  - direct **189 min**;
  - density-fitted **43 min**;
  - SOSCF 77 min.
- **Scaling that to B3LYP/def2-TZVPD with DF:** ×(2220/1800)³ ≈ 1.9, plus
  XC grid work, gives **~1.5–2.5 h of SCF**. Add the file write at
  N 2220 (caffeine's TZVPD write was 192 s on L at N 614; expect tens of
  minutes).
- **DF memory:** the 3-index tensor is 4500 aux × N²/2 × 8 B =
  **89 GB at TZVPD**. That is in core only on XL (244 GB), out-of-core on
  L.
- **The price:** C₆₀/TZVPD is a 3–4 h XL on-demand job, **≈ $6–8, the
  whole month's meter**, for a picture that def2-SVP draws equally well.
  C₆₀'s frontier orbitals (h_u HOMO, t₁u LUMO) are qualitatively identical
  at SVP. Diffuse functions matter for anions and Rydberg states, not for
  a neutral fullerene's valence MOs.
- **def2-SVP direct:**
  - sizing v4 gives (4.84 + 42 000·0.84³·⁵)/11.1 ≈ **2 060 s SCF on L**,
    plus ~10–15 min of files;
  - working set ≈ 3.3 GB;
  - **~45–50 min**, and 1.5 × that exceeds a 1 h single ceiling.
- **def2-SVP with DF:** ~15–20 min, with a 13 GB cderi tensor that fits on
  L.
- **Rule** (new in the next sizing version): when recipe A's TZVPD
  prediction is refused as too long, offer **the same recipe at def2-SVP**
  as a labelled fallback. Caption: "Basis reduced to def2-SVP to fit the
  worker; orbital shapes are qualitatively the same, energies shift by
  ~0.1 eV." Do not silently swap.

## 6. Recommended v1 scope

**Recipe C `scan`, canonical job fields** (hashed like A and B):

- `coordinate: {type: bond|angle|dihedral, atoms: [1-based]}`
- `mode: relaxed|rigid`
- `values: [...]`: an explicit grid, generated from start/stop/points in
  the UI, so the hash is exact
- `method: {opt: "b3lyp-d3bj/def2-svp", energy: "b3lyp-d3bj/def2-tzvp" | null, df: true}`
- `reference: rks|uks-bs`
- `charge`, `spin`, the geometry, and `sizing_version`

**Allowed in v1:**

- **Relaxed dihedral** (the main case): the default grid is 24 × 15°
  (−180…165). Other grids: 12 × 30°, 36 × 10°, or a half-turn 0–180 for
  symmetric rotors (13 points). Refused if the scanned bond is in a ring,
  or the dihedral has a near-linear angle.
- **Relaxed angle:** 13 points by default, up to 25, capped at 175°.
- **Relaxed bond (polyatomic):** 20 points, dense near R_e and sparse
  outward, from 0.85·R₀ to 2.5·R₀. UKS-BS when the bond is between
  heavy atoms or is to be broken.
- **Rigid:** any coordinate. For a dihedral it rotates the smaller
  fragment (refused for ring bonds); for an angle it bends about the
  vertex; for a bond it translates the smaller fragment. A diatomic's bond
  scan is always rigid (it is identical).
- **Limits:**
  - ≤ 36 points;
  - relaxed scans need N_SVP ≤ 465 (in core on L, roughly ≤ 25 heavy
    atoms);
  - predicted cost ≤ $1.00;
  - predicted wall time per chain ≤ 2 h × 1/1.5 headroom;
  - ≤ 4 chains.
  - Anything else is refused with the reason, as recipes A and B do.

**Execution:**

- One parent job record. Batch array size = number of chains (1–4); a
  rigid scan has one child per batch of ~10 points.
- Each child is one sequential warm chain, using one reused gradient
  scanner and `symmetry=False`.
- Each point is a `$set` constrained optimisation with `maxsteps` 50 per
  point.
- Each child checks the achieved constraint and writes
  `points/{i}.json` + `points/{i}.xyz` to S3 as each point finishes.
- After the chains: TZVP single points (when sized in) run on the same
  children. A small `merge` step (in the last child to finish, or the
  reconcile Lambda) writes `profile.csv`, `profile.json` and `scan.xyz`.
- It also writes orbital/density files at key points: the global minimum,
  the global maximum, and the two ends. For **every** point instead when
  N_TZVP ≤ 250, which covers the teaching set.

**Sizing:**

- Reuse v4's laws: an in-core step uses `t3Step`, the single point uses
  `t3`.
- Add a DF regime (a new constant, from one probe after pitfall 3 is
  fixed).
- Count steps as `min(10 + 2·atoms, 30)` for each chain's seed + 20 per
  later point (measured, §4.3).
- The meter reserves for all children (Spot × 3 attempts each, as now).
- Record per-point steps and cycles, for v6.

**Outputs:**

- `profile.csv` columns: `index, value, unit, achieved, E_h, dE_kcal,
  dE_kJ, converged, steps, scf_cycles, s2, chain, flag`.
- `profile.json`: the above, plus the point group and orbital energies per
  point (with labels), stationary points (minima/maxima by neighbour
  comparison) and barrier heights (each maximum minus the lower adjacent
  minimum, in both units), and a "hysteresis" list.
- `scan.xyz`: multi-frame, with comment `"<coord>=<value> E=<Eh>"`.
- Plus `input.py` (it reruns one point or the whole chain), `output.log`
  and `job.json`.

**UI essentials (owner-only request, public result like A/B):**

1. **Coordinate picker.** Click 2/3/4 atoms; the type follows from the
   count, and the current value is shown.
2. **Grid.** Start/stop/points with a live grid preview and the spacing.
   Presets: "full rotation 24 × 15°", "quick 12 × 30°", "bond
   0.85–2.5 R₀". Also a relaxed/rigid toggle with its caveat ("rigid
   overestimates barriers").
3. **Preview.** Method line, sizes, chains, predicted time and cost, and
   the refusal reason if refused.
4. **Result view.**
   - The profile plot, with the unit toggle (kcal/mol ⇄ kJ/mol, and Eh in
     the tooltip).
   - Minima and maxima marked, and ΔE‡ labelled.
   - Points flagged unconverged or hysteresis drawn hollow.
   - A slider snapping to points, with play/pause animation of the 3-D
     structure. This reuses Bonds mode's snapping-slider pattern and its
     "nothing between points was calculated" caption.
5. **Walsh panel:** orbital energies vs coordinate, with symmetry labels
   where the point group allows. Clicking a level highlights that orbital
   at the key points that have files.
6. **"Open this point as a molecule"** submits recipe A at that geometry
   (with dedupe), to get full orbitals anywhere on the curve.
7. **Exports:** the CSV, the multi-frame XYZ, and the PNG of the plot.
